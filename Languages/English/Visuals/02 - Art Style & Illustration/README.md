## Introduction

This covers creating art assets for STS2.
* Since STS2 is still in beta, this tutorial will be updated periodically.
* This tutorial is aimed at people with some art background who want to try hand-drawn assets. Zero-baseline beginners can try as well — drawing itself is a joyful thing.

## Preparation

If you've never drawn before, you don't necessarily need to skip this chapter. Drawing isn't difficult — on the contrary, it's an enjoyable process.

Here's a checklist of tools and software for illustration work.

* **Drawing hardware:**
	1. `Graphics tablet + pen` (classic tablet setup — even entry-level ~$15 ones cover all tablet drawing needs. Seated posture is easy on the back and neck.)
	2. `Pen display + pen` (Pen displays are easier to control than tablets, but pricier. Cheap ones have noticeable color inaccuracy.)
	3. `iPad + Apple Pencil` (the most beginner-friendly option — colors and responsiveness are excellent.)

	Check Bilibili for hardware review videos. This is just a brief overview.

* **Drawing software:**
	1. `Photoshop` (the classic. Subscription at ~$10/month. Pirated versions occasionally force-quit — annoying.)
	2. `CSP` (Clip Studio Paint — used by professional illustrators. One-time purchase, feature-complete.)
	3. `Krita` (actively maintained, free and open-source. Has some minor bugs.)
	4. `Procreate` (iPad only, ~$10 on the App Store. Simple and easy to pick up.)

	*Same as above — check Bilibili for recommendations. But just like switching phones, once you learn one piece of software, switching to another only takes a couple of days to get comfortable. So don't worry about starting over.*

The above covers hand-drawn illustration. You can also create art through other methods — extracting game files, AI generation, etc. There are many ways to create without drawing.

## Drawing in the STS2 Art Style

* If you have AI or extracted game assets, you can skip this section.
* I'll use `Krita` to paint an illustration:

![](../../images/1.webp)

Step one: take a screenshot in-game as reference. This helps maintain visual consistency and avoids a jarring look.

![](../../images/2.webp)

Also gather art reference for the character you want to draw. I recommend `PureRef 2.0` (a reference image tool). You can find installation guides on Bilibili.

![](../../images/11.webp)

Lower the opacity of the in-game screenshot and start sketching. At this stage, get the proportions between the character and enemies right.

![](../../images/6.webp)

Then build on the sketch by analyzing and drawing out the character's defining features.

* For STS2, I recommend exaggerating the character's outfit. Compared to earlier reference material, I've exaggerated the hat while removing details like lace trim and pendants.
* A character's hairstyle matters a lot in an anime-inspired style. Facial features are secondary. (It's like recognizing someone from far away — you identify them by height, build, and clothing, not their face.)
* Don't draw too many distinct lines. STS2's art leans toward a Western cartoon style built with color blocks. Too many lines will get in the way later.

![](../../images/10.webp)

Boldly block in the colors like this. Some distortion in perspective and proportions is fine — it actually fits STS2's comic style better.

![](../../images/8.webp)

Finally, keep cutting in shadows and highlights with color blocks. Once the volume reads well, add a few details and you're done.

## Notes

* The character shouldn't wear too much clothing (literally — don't overthink it). The artwork will later be broken down and rigged for animation. Clothes and hair need physics simulation. Too much hair or overly long garments will eat up a lot of debugging time.
* So consider designing characters with form-fitting outfits or mecha-style designs.
* Don't obsess over lighting and shadows while painting. STS2's in-game lighting is rarely dramatic. Strong contrast will look out of place.
